OutcomeThree 4″ × 4″ models
Learning Objectives
  • Reading a landform term as a shape a body moves through
  • Letting found material suggest form
  • Working fast and small
AssignmentThree 4″ × 4″ study models, each of a different landform term, made in class from material you find in the school.
The studio starts with words. Each term below names a landform, and each one already describes a way a body climbs, rests or passes. Pick three and make each one in your hands within the class.
Use whatever the building gives you. The material is half the idea: a folded sheet makes a different ridge than a pile of sand.
01 Pick Three Terms
Choose three terms from the list, each different in kind (a raised form, a carved form, a stepped form, for example).
  • Mound, a raised heap of ground
  • Berm, a long low bank
  • Terrace, a level step cut into a slope
  • Ramp, a steady incline
  • Cut, ground removed to make a passage
  • Fill, ground added to raise a level
  • Ridge, a long crest between two slopes
  • Valley, a low passage between two slopes
  • Saddle, the low point between two high points
  • Bowl, ground that falls toward a center
  • Plateau, a raised level plane
  • Ledge, a narrow shelf on a steep face
  • Cliff, a near-vertical face
  • Switchback, a path that turns back on itself to climb
  • Swale, a shallow channel that carries water
02 Hunt for Material
Search the school for anything that holds a shape: cardboard, foam offcuts, paper, wire, sand, fabric, model-shop scraps. Ask before you take.
03 Make the Models
Build each term on a 4″ × 4″ base. Spend about half an hour on each, then move on. Write the term on the underside.
04 Photograph
Photograph each model from above and at eye level, on a plain background, and upload the photographs by Monday.
Due. Bring all three models to S03 (Thu 2/4); the catalog starts from them.
